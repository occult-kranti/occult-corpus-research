# Literature Review: Online Occult Communities & Computational Folkloristics

Compiled for the *Occult Corpus Research* project. All citations in ACM format.

---

## 1. Computational Folkloristics — Foundational Methods

The field of **computational folkloristics** provides our primary methodological framework. This interdisciplinary approach combines large-scale text analysis, network analysis, and machine learning to study folklore corpora.

### 1.1 Core Papers

**[Tangherlini2012]** J. Abello, P. Broadwell, and T.R. Tangherlini. "Computational folkloristics." *Communications of the ACM*, 55(7), 2012. 147 citations. https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2209249.2209267

> *Key insight:* The availability of substantial digital folklore corpora creates the need for computational methods. The authors propose network-based approaches to analyze motif relationships across thousands of folktales.

**[Tangherlini2016]** T.R. Tangherlini. "Big Folklore: A Special issue on computational folkloristics." *Journal of American Folklore*, 129(511), 2016. 65 citations. https://muse.jhu.edu/pub/25/article/613891/summary

> *Key insight:* Introduces the concept of "story networks" — bipartite graphs connecting stories to their constituent motifs, characters, and locations. Enables latent pattern detection at scale.

**[Broadwell2016]** P.M. Broadwell and T.R. Tangherlini. "WitchHunter: Tools for the geo-semantic exploration of a Danish Folklore Corpus." *Journal of American Folklore*, 129(511), 2016. 18 citations.

> *Key insight:* Presents tools for visualizing semantic connections between stories in large corpora (>30,000 stories). Demonstrates that computational methods can reveal patterns invisible to close reading.

**[Abello2023]** J. Abello, P.M. Broadwell, T.R. Tangherlini, and H. Zhang. "Disentangling the folklore hairball: A network approach to the characterization of a large folktale corpus." *Fabula*, 64(1-2), 2023. 6 citations. https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/fabula-2023-0004/html

> *Key insight:* Proposes a multi-layer network model for folktale corpora, separating story-topic, story-motif, and story-place relationships into distinct network layers.

### 1.2 Methodological Foundations

**[Karsdorp2016]** F.B. Karsdorp. "Retelling stories. A computational-evolutionary perspective." PhD thesis, Radboud University, 2016. 17 citations.

> *Key insight:* Applies computational-evolutionary models to study how stories mutate and spread. Provides tools for analyzing the structure and evolution of narrative networks.

**[Lendvai2010]** P. Lendvai, T. Declerck, S. Darányi, et al. "Integration of linguistic markup into semantic models of folk narratives." *LREC*, 2010. 28 citations.

> *Key insight:* Demonstrates how to integrate linguistic annotation (POS tagging, named entities) with semantic models of folk narratives for computational analysis.

## 2. Digital Occultism & Online Witchcraft Communities

Research specifically examining occult/witchcraft communities on social media and forums.

### 2.1 Digital Ethnographies

**[Obadia2023]** L. Obadia. "'Spiritual' witchcraft and magic 2.0 as weapons of resistance: The emergence of a new movement?" *Social Compass*, 70(4), 2023. 7 citations. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/00377686231217194

> *Key insight:* Studies the presence of witches on large platforms like Reddit and Instagram. Identifies 50 online communities (sites, blogs, hashtags). Frames digital witchcraft as a form of resistance.

**[Obadia2026]** L. Obadia. "Witchy apps: Digital witchcraft and technological multimodality." *Social Compass*, 2026. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/00377686261446524

> *Key insight:* Examines the r/witchyapps subreddit community, analyzing how digital tools mediate witchcraft practice and community formation.

**[Jonsson2022]** F. Jonsson. "The Instagram witch: An ethnographic study about practicing witches in contemporary postsecular Sweden." MA thesis, Lund University, 2022. 4 citations.

> *Key insight:* Digital ethnography combining Instagram observation with Reddit forum analysis. Examines how witchcraft communities negotiate identity online.

**[Baybayan2024]** P.A.A. Baybayan and K.D.J. Orlina. "From Folklore to Online Spaces: A Digital Ethnographic Study Among Philippine Witches." 2024.

> *Key insight:* Multi-platform ethnography (Spotify, Facebook, Reddit, Twitter) of Philippine witchcraft communities. Demonstrates the value of cross-platform comparison.

### 2.2 Magical Discourse & Rhetoric

**[Gunn2011]** J. Gunn. *Modern occult rhetoric: Mass media and the drama of secrecy in the twentieth century.* University of Alabama Press, 2011. 109 citations.

> *Key insight:* Treats the occult as a rhetorical phenomenon tied to language and symbols. Examines how occult discourse functions as an idiom for expressing social anxieties.

**[Lehrich2007]** C.I. Lehrich. *The occult mind: magic in theory and practice.* Cornell University Press, 2007. 81 citations.

> *Key insight:* Analytical study of occult, magical, and esoteric traditions. Provides theoretical framework for analyzing magical texts as rhetorical artifacts.

**[Miles2008]** C. Miles. "Occult Retraction: Cornelius Agrippa and the Paradox of Magical Language." *Rhetoric Society Quarterly*, 38(4), 2008. 15 citations.

> *Key insight:* Analyzes the linguistic paradigm in the Corpus Hermeticum. Demonstrates how magical language functions through paradox and retraction.

**[Hanegraaff2023]** W.J. Hanegraaff and M. Mukhopadhyay. "Translating Esotericism: Scepticism, Optimism, Agency." *Correspondences*, 11(1), 2023. 10 citations.

> *Key insight:* Discusses corpus-based approaches to esoteric vocabulary. Proposes translation methodologies for esoteric texts.

## 3. Network Analysis of Knowledge Communities

Methodological papers on analyzing subcultural communities through network methods.

**[Fine1979]** G.A. Fine and S. Kleinman. "Rethinking subculture: An interactionist analysis." *American Journal of Sociology*, 85(1), 1979. 795 citations.

> *Key insight:* Foundational theory distinguishing between subculture (values/behavior) and subsociety (structural membership). Essential for understanding online occult communities.

**[DiMaggio2011]** P. DiMaggio. "Cultural networks." In *The Sage Handbook of Social Network Analysis*, 2011. 207 citations.

> *Key insight:* Reviews network studies of cultural production and consumption. Provides framework for analyzing how knowledge circulates in subcultural communities.

**[Jewson2007]** N. Jewson. "Cultivating network analysis: Rethinking the concept of 'community' within 'communities of practice'." In *Communities of Practice: Critical Perspectives*, 2007. 163 citations.

> *Key insight:* Argues that network analysis can reveal subcultural clusters that express variant forms of dominant norms. Relevant for identifying sub-traditions within occult communities.

**[Tuire2001]** P. Tuire and L. Erno. "Exploring invisible scientific communities: Studying networking relations within an educational research community." *Higher Education*, 42(4), 2001. 166 citations.

> *Key insight:* Demonstrates how social network analysis reveals subcultural divisions within communities. Methodological template for our network analysis.

## 4. Topic Modeling & Fringe Community Analysis

Papers using topic modeling and NLP to study fringe/alt communities online.

**[Rieger2021]** D. Rieger, A.S. Kümpel, M. Wich, et al. "Assessing the extent and types of hate speech in fringe communities: A case study of alt-right communities on 8chan, 4chan, and Reddit." *Social Media + Society*, 7(4), 2021. 208 citations.

> *Key insight:* Combines manual coding with topic modeling to analyze discourse in fringe communities. Methodological template for our topic modeling pipeline.

**[Klein2018]** C. Klein, P. Clutton, and V. Polito. "Topic modeling reveals distinct interests within an online conspiracy forum." *Frontiers in Psychology*, 9, 2018. 91 citations.

> *Key insight:* Uses LDA topic modeling to identify distinct interest clusters within a conspiracy forum. Demonstrates that topic modeling can reveal latent thematic structure.

**[Yang2023]** T. Yang and K. Fang. "How dark corners collude: A study on an online Chinese alt-right community." *Information, Communication & Society*, 26(5), 2023. 48 citations.

> *Key insight:* Combines topic modeling, social network analysis, and discourse analysis. Provides a multi-method template for studying fringe online communities.

**[Hoseini2024]** M. Hoseini, P. de Freitas Melo, F. Benevenuto, et al. "Characterizing information propagation in fringe communities on Telegram." *Proceedings of ICWSM*, 2024. 22 citations.

> *Key insight:* Uses transformer-based embeddings (BERTopic) for topic modeling in fringe communities. Demonstrates superiority of neural topic models over LDA.

## 5. Digital Folklore & Platform Studies

**[Zhang2025]** H. Zhang, Y. Belinskaya, D.A. Rodgers, et al. "Algorithmic Alchemy: The Power of the Witch on YouTube." *Folklore*, 2025.

> *Key insight:* Argues that in the age of "big folklore," computational methods are essential. Examines how magical belief systems manifest on YouTube.

**[Dorst2016]** J.D. Dorst. "Folklore's cybernetic imaginary, or, unpacking the obvious." *Journal of American Folklore*, 129(512), 2016. 18 citations.

> *Key insight:* Critically examines the digital/computational turn in folklore studies. Important for situating our work within disciplinary debates.

## Research Gap Analysis

After reviewing this literature, several gaps emerge that our project addresses:

1. **Cross-platform comparison:** Most studies focus on a single platform (Reddit OR Instagram OR YouTube). No study compares mainstream (Reddit) vs. fringe (dedicated occult forums) communities.

2. **Longitudinal analysis:** Existing studies are typically synchronic (snapshot at one time). Our dataset spans 5+ years, enabling temporal analysis.

3. **Combined NLP + network + discourse:** While individual methods exist, no study combines BERTopic topic modeling, social network analysis of reply structures, AND rhetorical/discourse analysis on occult communities.

4. **Open dataset:** Most studies use private or restricted data. We will release an anonymized open dataset.

5. **Computational folkloristics on living traditions:** Computational folkloristics has focused on historical archives. Our work applies these methods to *living, evolving* online communities.
